I · The house

Maison Vérault — Haute horlogerie

We do not make objects
for everyone.
We make them for the few
who already know.

A private maison in Genève since 1891. Timepieces assembled by a single pair of hands. Fragrances composed in the dark. Gold, worked until it forgets it was ever metal.

Twelve pieces leave the bench each year. They do not travel in padded envelopes. They fall, once, into a coffret made for that movement alone — then the lid closes itself.

II · Anatomy of the packing

Twelve gestures
between air and wax.

  1. 01 · Release

    The watchmaker opens the fingers. Orion is no longer held. This is not carelessness. It is the last decision of the bench.

  2. 02 · Rotation

    In the fall the case turns once, slowly, so the lamp can read the gold one last time in the open air.

  3. 03 · Hover

    Above the closed coffret the piece waits. Gravity is asked to pause. The box has not yet agreed to open.

  4. 04 · Hinge

    The lid lifts on a hidden gold pin. No hand touches it. The house prefers the box to remember how.

  5. 05 · Light

    Suede takes the lamp. The circular well — cut for forty-one millimeters and nothing else — becomes visible.

  6. 06 · Descent

    Orion lowers. The strap folds. The case finds the well as if the suede had been waiting with a name.

  7. 07 · Nest

    The watch lies flat. Hands still. The crystal looks at the lid, not at us.

  8. 08 · Close

    The lid returns. Gold edge meets gold edge. The sound is smaller than a breath.

  9. 09 · Ribbon

    Champagne silk crosses the calf. It is not decoration. It is a sentence the house writes without ink.

  10. 10 · Wax

    A seal, still warm, takes the letter V. Not a trademark. A lock that can be broken only once.

  11. 11 · Rest

    The coffret sits. The room is quiet enough to hear the movement decide it is finished.

  12. 12 · Letter

    Only then is the name written. The box does not travel without a sentence addressed to one person.

IV · Principles

What the house
will not do.

No catalogue

A catalogue is a promise to strangers. We keep a bench, not a brochure. If a piece exists, it has a name and a year. If it does not, we do not sketch it for you.

No queue

A waiting list is a crowd wearing patience. We prefer a letter. Some letters are answered. Most are thanked and left in the drawer.

No second hands

The pair that begins a movement is the pair that lets it fall. Outsourcing the last gesture would be a kind of lie the lamp would notice.

No water stories

Orion is not a diver. Rain is permitted. The ocean is not a personality. We do not print depths we do not love.

No loud gold

Champagne, not yellow. The metal should look as if it cooled in a quiet room. If it shouts, it is not ours.

No open boxes

A coffret that arrives already undone is a shop. Ours pack themselves. The seal is meant to be broken once, by one person.

VI · Calibre V-41

Every part has
a night attached.

The movement is not a list of specifications. It is a sequence of evenings. These are the names we use when the lamp is on.

Main plate
German silver, frosted, then left alone. We do not rhodium what the lamp already likes.
Barrels
Two. Seventy-two hours. Wound by a stem that still feels like a key.
Escapement
Lever, in-house, with an impulse we refuse to make “sporty.”
Balance
Variable inertia. Four gold weights. No regulator index to argue with.
Hairspring
Overcoil, counted by eye, not by a machine that cannot hear it.
Pallets
Stone, set, oiled once, then watched for a week as if they might change their mind.
Escape wheel
Polished until it disappears in the lamp and only the teeth remain.
Train
Four wheels. No date. Dates are for offices.
Bridges
Anglage by hand. Inside angles that a CNC would call inefficient and we call the point.
Chatons
Gold, for twenty-three jewels. Screwed, not pressed. Pressing is for pastry.
Screws
Blued where the steel can take heat. Gold where the plate asks for quiet.
Stem
Square, honest. The crown is a conversation, not a button.
Keyless works
Setting is deliberate. You cannot change the hour by accident. That is a feature of character.
Hands
Gold, fired, slim. They do not luminesce. Night is allowed to be dark.
Dial
Black sunburst. The grain runs out from a center that is not printed with a logo.
Crystal
Sapphire, boxed, both sides. You may look at the movement if you have earned the back.
Caseback
Held by screws, signed only with a V small enough to miss.
Gaskets
Enough for rain. Not enough for a story about trenches.
Lugs
Soldered, not bolted. The strap is a continuation of the case, not an accessory.
Buckle
Pin. Folding clasps feel like luggage.
Coffret well
Suede, circular, 41 mm and a breath. Cut after the case exists, never before.
Ribbon
Champagne silk. Tied after the lid, never before.
Seal
Wax, still warm. Broken once.
Letter
The last part of the calibre. Without it the box is only a box.

VI · The years

A house that learned
to wait.

1891

Élise Vérault opens a single bench on Rue des Granges. She repairs marine chronometers and refuses a sign.

1912

The first Orion is delivered in a black coffret. The lid is closed by the same hands that cut the pinion.

1947

The salon in Paris is a room, not a shop. Appointments are written in pencil and often erased.

1978

Nocturne appears — a skeleton for those who prefer the night to keep its machinery visible.

2004

Ambre Noir is composed for the atelier only. A few decanters leave. Most stay under the lamp.

2026

Twelve letters. Four pieces. The coffret still packs itself. The house has not learned to advertise.

IX · Quiet questions

Asked,
seldom answered.

Can I buy Orion online?

No. There is no cart. There is a letter. If the piece is meant for you, the house will say so.

Why does the watch fall?

Because placing it would be theatre. The last tool on the bench is gravity. The well is cut so precisely that the fall is a kind of yes.

How many pieces this year?

Twelve in total across the bench. Orion is among them. Nocturne is six. Sceau is one. Ambre is decanted, not counted like steel.

Do you service what you did not make?

Rarely. The lamp is busy with its own unfinished nights.

May I visit without a letter?

You may stand on Rue des Granges. The door will not argue with you. It will also not open.

Is Vérault a real maison?

It is a fictional house built as a study in quiet luxury — a website with many rooms, not a single page pretending to be a brand.
